## Email 2 – Day 2: Tip – tailor in 10 minutes

**Subject:** The 10-minute habit that gets more interview calls
**Preview text:** One small change before every application.

Hi {first_name},

Quick tip today, and it's the one most freshers skip.

**Tailor your resume for every job. It only takes 10 minutes.**

Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) and recruiters both look for the words used in the job description. If the post says "Bootstrap 5" and your resume says "CSS frameworks", you might get missed.

Here's the 10-minute routine:

1. **Copy the job description** into a notes app.
2. **Highlight the skills and tools** it mentions more than once.
3. **Update your Summary** so the first line matches the job title ("Front-End Developer", not "Aspiring IT professional").
4. **Reorder your Skills** so the matching ones come first. Only add skills you genuinely have.
5. **Save a copy** named for that company, e.g. *Priya-Sharma-Resume-AcmeTech.pdf*.

That's it. Same resume, better match.

Tip: keep a master version with everything, and make tailored copies from it.

Talk soon,
Nilesh

## Email 3 – Day 4: Tip – bullets that prove it

**Subject:** Turn "worked on" into "built, improved, reduced"
**Preview text:** A simple formula for resume bullets that stand out.

Hi {first_name},

Let's fix the most common weak spot on fresher resumes: the bullet points.

**Weak:** "Worked on the college website."
**Strong:** "Rebuilt the college events page in HTML and Bootstrap, making it mobile-friendly and cutting page load time from 6s to 2s."

The formula:

> **Action verb + what you did + tool/skill + result (with a number if you can)**

Good action verbs: *built, designed, improved, reduced, automated, led, launched, organised, tested, documented.*

No numbers? Try these:

- How many people used it? (classmates, users, visitors)
- How much time did it save?
- How many pages, screens or features did you build?
- What score improved? (Lighthouse, test marks, ranking)

Pick your top 2 projects today and rewrite one bullet for each using the formula. It's a small change that makes your resume read like someone who gets things done.
