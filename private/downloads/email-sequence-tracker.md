## Email 2 – Day 2: Tip – the follow-up

**Subject:** The follow-up email most applicants never send
**Preview text:** A short, polite template you can copy.

Hi {first_name},

Here's a simple way to stand out: **follow up.**

Most applicants apply and wait. A short, polite follow-up 5 to 7 days later shows you're genuinely interested, and sometimes it's what gets your application looked at.

That's what the **Follow-up date** column in your tracker is for. Set it when you apply. If the date passes and the job is still open, it turns **red**.

Here's a follow-up you can copy:

> Subject: Following up – [Role] application
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> I applied for the [Role] position on [date] and wanted to say I'm still very interested. I recently [one relevant thing, e.g. built a responsive dashboard in Bootstrap], and I think I could help with [something from the job post].
>
> Happy to share anything else that would be useful. Thanks for your time!
>
> [Your name]
> [Phone] | [Portfolio/LinkedIn]

Short, specific, friendly. One follow-up is enough. Don't send three.

Talk soon,
Nilesh

## Email 3 – Day 4: Tip – read your numbers

**Subject:** What your tracker is telling you
**Preview text:** Use the Summary tab to fix the right problem.

Hi {first_name},

Once you have 10 to 15 applications in your tracker, the **Summary** tab starts to tell you something useful. Here's how to read it:

- **Lots of "Applied", very few "Interview"?** Your resume probably isn't matching the jobs well enough. Tailor your Summary and Skills to each job description, and make sure your resume is ATS-friendly.
- **Interviews, but no offers?** Focus on interview prep. Practise explaining your projects out loud in 2 minutes each.
- **Not many applications at all?** Set a small weekly target, like 5 quality applications, and use the *Applications in last 7 days* number to stay on track.

Also look at the **Source** column. If most of your interviews come from referrals or company websites, spend more time there.

Keep going,
